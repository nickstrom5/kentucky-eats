Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the hollows.

What stays on your phone

Kentucky Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Kentucky Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Three kinds of source. The Hot Brown, Kentucky classics, bourbon country, Covington and Newport, historic inns and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a dated 2025 or 2026 source or a Kentucky tourism office's own page behind each place. Louisville Metro's permits, inspections and ABC licenses are public records. The wider directory of 8,678 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I get a Hot Brown?

Our Hot Brown list has 9 hand-checked places, from the Brown Hotel, where it was created in 1926, to Lou Lou's Hot Brown pizza and the Hot Brown soup on Frankfort's Hot Brown Trail. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Kentucky restaurant's inspection?

Kentucky scores each inspection out of 100 (the violation points subtracted from 100), and the state's public inspection portal has every county's latest scores. Louisville Metro also publishes its inspections as open data, so Louisville places in the app show their latest score, and Louisville's own A, B or C grade where it gave one, as published and dated. Every other place links to its county's table on the portal, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

In Louisville, yes: Louisville Metro publishes its alcoholic beverage licenses, so places with an on-premises license say so. Elsewhere, no: Kentucky ABC's statewide lookup posts no terms of use, so the app doesn't say either way, and dry or moist counties can still vote in restaurant sales.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
